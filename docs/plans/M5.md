# M5 · Traversal

**Governing document:** `CLAUDE.md` §6 M5, §2.5, §2.6, §5, §11
**Status:** plan. No code until this is signed off (§7.2).

> *"new `vehicle.js` — hover craft and short-hop flyer. Continuous enter/exit,
> camera inherits velocity. Dust, grass displacement, spray all sampling the M3
> field. Speed bounded by quadtree streaming rate."* — §6 M5

---

## 1 · What this milestone actually is

M4 gave the world a body. M5 gives it a **distance**. Everything before this has
been about a place you stand in; this is the first milestone about a place you
*cross*, and its gate is a single unbroken capture forty kilometres long.

The interesting engineering is not the vehicle. A hover craft is a spring, a
drag term and a yaw rate, and `src/traveler.js` already has one. The interesting
engineering is the sentence at the end of §6 M5 — **"speed bounded by quadtree
streaming rate"** — because that is the only clause in the milestone ladder that
makes a *gameplay* number a *derived* one, and because getting it wrong is
precisely the pop-in the gate forbids.

---

## 2 · What already exists

**`src/traveler.js` has a working hover skiff.** Not a stub: a hull with wings,
a fin and a keel light, docked near the town plaza, mounted with `E`, driven
with the movement axis, dismounted with `E`. It carries a six-symbol interface
against `surface.js` — `.place`, `.toggleView`, `.tryMount`, `.drive`, `.third`,
`.riding` — which the M4 plan deliberately preserved.

So M5 does not start from nothing. What the skiff is missing against §6 M5:

| §6 M5 asks for | skiff today |
|---|---|
| hover craft | ✅ |
| **short-hop flyer** | ❌ nothing |
| continuous enter/exit (§2.5) | partial — mounts by teleporting the body to the skiff |
| **camera inherits velocity** | ❌ the rig re-aims, it does not carry momentum |
| dust / grass displacement / spray | ❌ none, and until M3 there was no field to sample |
| **speed bounded by streaming rate** | ❌ unbounded |

Three of those six are the milestone. The other three are polish on something
that works.

**`src/quadtree.js` is the constraint.** It splits when `dist < chord · splitK`
with `splitK = 6.5`, runs a worker pool of 2–4, and holds a fixed cache cap of
900 tiles (600 on glass). Its `stats` already reports `pending`, which is the
number M5's speed bound has to read.

**And forty kilometres do not fit on the tile.** This is the correction that
reorganises the milestone. `surface.js` is an authored 1400 m patch — it clamps
the body to `±EXT·0.48`, i.e. 672 m from the middle (`surface.js:2165`). The
gate's *"40 km traverse"* can only happen on the streaming globe in
`planetscale.js`, which is where the quadtree, `maxDepth = 18` and the whole
pop-in question live. So M5 lands in **two places**, and the split is not
arbitrary — each half owns the clauses it can actually satisfy:

| §6 M5 clause | where it lands | why there |
|---|---|---|
| 40 km traverse, **no pop-in**, speed bounded by streaming | `planetscale.js` | the quadtree is there and nowhere else |
| **dust, grass displacement, spray** sampling the M3 field | `surface.js` / `traveler.js` | the wind field and the meadow are there and nowhere else |
| hover craft, short-hop flyer, continuous enter/exit, camera inherits velocity | both | one `vehicle.js`, two consumers |

The unit systems differ and that is load-bearing, not incidental: the surface
tile is in metres, the globe is in draw units at `R = 2600` with
`unitKm = radiusE·6371/2600` (2.45 km per unit on an Earth-sized world, so the
2.2 m eye height is `eyeH = 0.0009`). §2.6 is why: a float32 world position at
planet scale is forbidden, and the two paths keep their own coordinate handling
and share only the dynamics.

## 3 · The speed bound, derived — then corrected by measurement

This is the part worth doing properly, because a hand-picked top speed is a
number that silently rots every time the tile resolution or the worker count
changes — and its failure mode is the gate's own forbidden word.

**This section was wrong in its first draft, by a factor of four.** The
correction is kept in view rather than quietly overwritten, because the way it
was caught is the whole argument for catching it: the first version was an
argument, and an argument is not a measurement.

### 3.1 · The first draft, and how it failed

The draft said: a tile at depth `d` has chord `c(d) = R·(π/2)/2^d`, splits when
the camera is nearer than `c(d)·splitK`, so new tiles arrive along a front
`2·splitK·c(d)` wide and

```
N(v, d) ≈ 2·splitK·v / c(d)          ← wrong, low by ~4×
```

To check it, `quadtree.js`'s own `visit()` walk was re-implemented in plain
numbers, a camera was flown along a great circle at fixed altitude and speed, and
the tiles it newly required were **counted** (`scratchpad/demand.mjs`). Across
twelve flights the measured rate was 2.1–4.3× the formula, clustering on 4.0 at
exactly the low, fast end where the bound has to hold.

Three candidates, each falsifiable:

| hypothesis | prediction | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (a) every depth contributes a front, not only the deepest | ×2 | **confirmed** — the per-level counts halve outward (337, 234, 122, 65, 30, 17 per second at depths 18→13) and sum to a geometric ×2 |
| (b) a depth-`d` tile is required when its *parent* splits, so its radius is `(2·splitK+1)·c(d)`, not `splitK·c(d)` | ×2 | **confirmed** — this is what `dist < chord·splitK` with `dist = near − R·ang` actually says, read one level up |
| (c) relief: flying over mountains breathes the camera's altitude and every split radius with it | ? | **falsified** — a near-flat world (amp 0.02) gives 813/s against the real world's 816/s |

(a) and (b) together give ×4, which is the observed factor.

### 3.2 · What the demand law actually is

```
c(d)       = R·(π/2) / 2^d                       tile chord at depth d
ρ(d)       = (2·splitK + 1) · c(d)               radius out to which depth d is required
N          = C · v / c_eff(alt)                  tiles demanded per second
c_eff(alt) = max( alt / (2·splitK+1), c(maxDepth) )
C          = 4·√2·(2·splitK + 1)
```

The `4` is (a) and (b). The `√2` is the last 42%, and it is not a fudge: it is a
**grid crossed on the diagonal.** A straight line over a square lattice enters
`(|cos θ| + |sin θ|)` cells per cell-width, worst case √2 at 45°, and a great
circle meets the cube-face grid at whatever angle it likes.

The tell that this is structure and not curve-fitting: sweeping 192 flights at
each of two split thresholds — 6.5 (desktop) and 5.2 (glass, `quadtree.js:607`) —
the worst-case ratio `C / 4(2·splitK+1)` comes out **1.408** and **1.422**. The
same number, at two different `splitK`, is what says the parametrisation is
right and only one constant ever needed measuring.

`c_eff` has a **floor**, and the floor is the interesting part. Above ~534 m the
deepest resident chord tracks altitude, so the bound is linear in it. Below that
the tree hits `maxDepth = 18` and cannot refine further, so the bound stops
falling and flattens. **That floor is the only reason a low hover craft is
possible at all** — without a maximum depth the speed limit would go to zero as
you approached the ground.

### 3.3 · The bound

```
v_max = W · c_eff(alt) / (C · τ)
```

Three things follow, and each is a check:

1. **The bound is tightest near the ground**, because `c_eff` shrinks with
   altitude until it floors. So the speed limit is a function of how close to the
   ground you are — which is correct, and is also what "a short-hop flyer" is
   *for*. Climbing is how you go fast.
2. **`τ` is measured, not assumed.** The draft guessed 25 ms. Timing `buildTile`
   directly (`scratchpad/tau.mjs`, 40 builds per depth) gives **8.6–11.6 ms** on
   this container, mean 9.8 — and it *falls* with depth, 11.6 ms at depth 4
   against 8.6 at depth 18, because a deep tile's 1089 height samples all land in
   one small neighbourhood. That is the favourable direction: τ is smallest
   exactly where the bound bites. It is still a per-machine number and a phone
   will not see 9.8 ms, so it is measured at init from the real worker
   round-trip. §11's *"adaptive quality mid-frame"* trap does not apply — this is
   a rate, not a quality knob, and reading it changes nothing that is drawn.
3. **The bound is a soft ceiling, not a hard clamp.** A vehicle that hits an
   invisible wall at speed is worse than one that pops a tile. The shape is a
   drag term that rises as the craft approaches the bound and as `pending`
   approaches the cap, so the craft *feels* the streaming pressure — the same
   move as `windForceScale` making an airless world's grass stand still without a
   branch.

### 3.4 · What the bound finds

**The flight law that ships today is already over it.** `planetscale.js:1514`
reads `spd = clamp(alt·0.8, 0.008, 1600)` units/s with a ×3.4 boost — "the
throttle is your altitude", which has exactly the right *shape*, linear in
altitude, and a constant nobody checked:

| workers | τ | today, unboosted | with boost | bound | worst over |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 9.8 ms | 320 m/s @ 400 m | 1088 m/s | 278 m/s | **3.9×** |
| 4 | 25 ms | 320 m/s | 1088 m/s | 109 m/s | **10.0×** |
| 2 | 25 ms | 320 m/s | 1088 m/s | 55 m/s | **19.9×** |
| 2 | 40 ms | 960 m/s @ 1200 m | 3264 m/s | 77 m/s | **42.6×** |

Over-driving the bound does not hole the ground — `quadtree.js:298` keeps the
parent drawn until all four children are resident, so the failure mode is that
the ground under you simply **stays coarse, and then pops a level when it
catches up.** Which is the gate's forbidden word, arrived at from the other
direction.

Worth saying plainly: the existing constant is wrong by 2.2× even *unboosted* on
a four-worker machine, and the boost is what turns that into an order of
magnitude. The fix is not a smaller constant. It is a bound that knows how many
workers this machine has and how fast they actually are.

## 4 · Continuity — §2.5 is the hard invariant here

> *"No cuts, no loading screens, no fades to black… If a feature can't be
> entered continuously, it isn't finished."*

The skiff today teleports the body onto the deck. That is a cut, and it has been
one since it was written; nobody noticed because the camera is behind the walker
and the jump is short. At 40 km it will not stay unnoticed.

The port is the one `transition.js` already implements for scale changes: the
mount is a **hyperzoom under a passing snapshot**, but at human scale it is
simpler — a spring that carries the eye from where it is to where it will be,
over about 350 ms, with the camera's own velocity preserved through the handover.

**Camera inherits velocity** is the same clause seen from the other side. When
you dismount at 60 m/s the body has to leave with the craft's momentum, and the
camera has to keep looking where it was looking. M4's `CameraRig` already
implements the duck-typed `{enabled, target, update()}` contract, so the handover
is a target swap, not a rebuild — but the *velocity* has to cross with it.

## 5 · Everything the craft disturbs samples the M3 field

M3 act 6 established `sampleWind(x, z, height)` as the one reading, and act 6's
suite asserts every consumer agrees. M5 adds three more consumers and they must
join that list rather than growing a fourth wind:

- **Dust** kicked up under the hover skirt. It is *in* the boundary layer, so it
  gets the gusts, and it must drift downwind after the craft has passed rather
  than settling straight down.
- **Grass displacement** under and behind the craft. `meadowPart()` already takes
  a walker with a radius and a push; a skiff is the same function with a larger
  radius and a push driven by its skirt pressure rather than by a gait phase.
  **The uniform is already there** — this is a caller change, not a shader one.
- **Spray**, over water, from the M2 act 5 ocean. The wave field and the wind
  field are already the same wind; spray is where a viewer can see that they are.

**Acts, in order** (§7.4 — each behind `?m5=1`, default-off):

1. **The governor.** `vehicle.js` + the τ measurement in `quadtree.js` + the
   offline suite. Nothing visible changes; the number becomes knowable.
2. **Wire it.** `planetscale.js`'s flight law reads the bound. This is the
   pop-in clause and the 40 km.
3. **The handover.** Continuous mount/dismount with momentum crossing both ways,
   camera inherits velocity, and the short-hop flyer.
4. **What the craft disturbs.** Dust, spray and grass displacement, all through
   `sampleWind` (§5 above).

`src/collision.js` **is not related and must not be touched** — it is a Toomre &
Toomre galaxy-collision GPU sim and the name is already spent. This trap has now
been recorded twice; the third time it will cost someone a day.

## 7 · Offline validation (§7.3)

A new `vehicle` suite:

- **The governor against the quadtree's own walk.** Not against the closed form —
  against a re-implementation of `visit()` flown along real great circles, which
  is the independent second computation §7.3 asks for and is what found the ×4.
  The red line: over a sweep of routes, altitudes and speeds, measured demand
  never exceeds `C·v/c_eff`.
- **The constant is structure, not a fit.** `C / 4(2·splitK+1)` is the same at
  `splitK` 6.5 and 5.2 — assert it, because the day someone changes `splitK` for
  glass is the day a fitted constant would silently stop being a bound.
- **The floor.** `c_eff` flattens at `c(maxDepth)`, so `v_max` is bounded below
  and a hovering craft is possible; assert the bound is monotone in altitude and
  strictly positive at zero altitude.
- **The scaling.** Twice the workers, twice the speed; twice τ, half the speed.
- **The soft ceiling.** Drag rises smoothly with `pending/cap` and is exactly
  zero when the queue is empty — a governor that always drags is a governor that
  has become a top speed.
- **Momentum crosses the handover.** Mount at speed, dismount, and the body's
  velocity equals the craft's to the digit. Then the reverse.
- **§2.3.** The same trace at the same `dt` gives a bit-identical 40 km path, and
  halving the timestep lands within a stated distance rather than somewhere else.
- **§2.6 precision.** A 40 km traverse is exactly where a float32 world position
  stops having centimetres. The suite asserts the craft's position stays in
  float64 and that nothing derived from it is rounded before the camera-relative
  subtraction.
- **Continuity.** The mount spring's position and velocity are continuous at both
  ends — no step in either, sampled either side of the handover frame.

## 8 · Gate (§6 M5)

> *One unbroken capture: standing → vehicle → 40 km traverse → dismount →
> walking. No pop-in, no seam, no frame over budget.*

Decidable here: the governor's arithmetic, momentum conservation, determinism,
precision, and the continuity of the mount spring. **Not** decidable here: pop-in,
seams, and the frame budget — all three need real silicon and the 40 km route,
and they are the gate's actual clauses. Routed via `docs/GPU-RUN.md`.

---

## 9 · Budgets at risk (§5)

- **Streaming**, obviously, and it is the milestone's whole subject.
- **Grass.** M3 draws 3.33 M instanced blades at walking pace. At 60 m/s the
  chunk grid re-homes every frame and ring 3's 250 m chunks turn over 24× faster.
  The instance buffers do not move — roots are chunk-relative — but the
  `chunkInstances` sweep runs over 412 chunks a frame regardless, and that is CPU
  §5 budgets at 12 ms.
- **The wind field's origin snap.** At 60 m/s the render target's origin moves
  8.5 texels a frame at 256². The snap that removed the shimmer at walking pace
  does nothing at this speed; whether the field needs to advect with the craft
  rather than follow it is a question this milestone has to answer.

## 11 · Acts 1–2, as built

The governor exists and `planetscale.js`'s flight law reads it behind `?m5=1`.
What the build changed against the plan:

- **`C` is `4√2(2·splitK+1)`, and the √2 is the last thing that was understood.**
  §3.2 has the argument. It is worth restating that the bound comes out
  *asymptotically tight* — worst slack over 72 flights is 1.03× — which is what
  a correct supremum looks like and also means there is no margin in the bound
  itself. The margin lives in the governor's knee instead, at 0.72 of the
  bound, which is the right place for it: a bound should be true, and a
  ceiling should be comfortable, and those are different jobs.
- **`τ` needed an outlier clamp.** An 8% exponential average is not robust: one
  350 ms tile behind a garbage collection dragged τ from 12 ms to 39 ms, which
  is a craft slamming on the brakes for a hiccup. Samples are clamped to 2.5×
  the running value before mixing, so a genuine slowdown still gets there in a
  few samples and a spike moves it 12%.
- **`Mount` needed an epsilon.** `dur/n` summed `n` times does not land on `dur`,
  so an exact `t >= dur` test left the handover live one frame past its end
  carrying an offset of about 1e-31 m. Invisible, and exactly the sort of state
  machine that never quite retires.
- **The surface skiff needs no governor.** `surface.js` is a fixed mesh with no
  streaming, so there is nothing to outrun; its speed is bounded by the tile.
  The governor is planet-scale only, which is the same split as §2 above.

**One open question, stated rather than settled.** At orbital altitude the bound
comes out slightly *below* the existing 1600 units/s clamp on a fast machine,
and well below it on a slow one — because crossing the planet in four seconds
really does churn the entire tile set, and the demand figure of ~440 tiles/s is
honest. Whether that should govern is a different question from whether it is
true: at orbit an un-refined tile subtends a few pixels, and `splitK` is already
chosen for a ~4 px error, so what the governor prevents there may be invisible.
The conservative behaviour ships because the flag is default-off and being
slower than necessary is the recoverable mistake. **Deciding it needs eyes on a
real orbit-to-ground descent**, and it is routed to `docs/GPU-RUN.md` with the
rest of M5's visual clauses rather than guessed at here.

## 12 · Acts 3–4, as planned

**Act 3 — the handover.** `traveler.js:139`'s `tryMount` teleports the body onto
the deck; `vehicle.js`'s `Mount` and `handMomentum` replace it. The eye keeps
its position and its velocity across the swap in both directions, and the
skiff's ad-hoc integration in `drive()` is replaced by the `Hover` the suite
already tests. Space becomes the short hop while riding — the same
variable-height rule the walker uses, so the one thing a rider already knows
transfers intact. The offset is applied after whichever placer owns the camera,
which is `rig.place()` on foot and the traveler's own arm while riding.

**Act 4 — what the craft disturbs.** `MEADOW_PART_GLSL`'s `PART_R` is a
compile-time constant of 1.2 m, which is a walker; a skiff is the same function
at four metres. It becomes a uniform — the one shader change in the milestone,
and the reason act 4 re-gates. The wake in `traveler.js:189` drifts straight up
on `arand()`; it should drift *downwind*, through `surface.js`'s `sampleWind`,
which is the reading every other consumer already agrees on (M3 act 6). Over
water it is spray and the wave field is the same wind — which is where a viewer
can actually see that the field is shared.

## 10 · What this milestone does not do

- No flight model beyond a short hop. §4: the verbs are *travel* and *look*.
- No fuel, no damage, no landing gear. This is a place, not a game loop.
- No new scale. A vehicle is a way of crossing the surface scale, not a sixth one.
