## 1 · What was there before, and why restyling it would not have worked

`hud.js:133-280`: a fixed 92 px rosette, a spring slider and seven permanent
buttons. All of it worked by **synthesising keystrokes**:

```js
window.dispatchEvent(new KeyboardEvent('keydown', { code }))   // hud.js:161
```

So a thumb — which has a magnitude and a direction — arrived at the controller
as four booleans and a boost flag. **That is why walking on glass felt like
nothing**, and no amount of restyling the stick would have fixed it: the analog
part was destroyed at the first hop. M4's `src/input.js` is what gave it
somewhere to go, which is the whole reason the two milestones moved together.

Two further defects, both of which the old layer could not see:

- **`matchMedia('(pointer: coarse)')` was read once**, at `hud.js:124`, with no
  change listener. A hybrid touchscreen laptop mounted the touch layer *and*
  kept every keyboard listener live — which is M0's unfinished "stop desktop and
  mobile control layers mounting simultaneously," still open at M7.
- **`H` did not kill it.** `#touch` had no `.hud` class, so the global toggle
  missed it, and §8 axis 7 asks whether deleting the HUD entirely loses you
  orientation — a question you cannot ask if part of it will not go.

## 2 · The mistake the first version made, and the rule that came out of it

Worth recording because it is the more useful half of this milestone.

The first version covered the bottom 62% of the glass with two
`pointer-events: auto` zones and left them there on **every** scale. Three of the
six — cosmic, galaxy and system — steer with `OrbitControls`, which listens on
the *canvas*. The zones swallowed every touch those scales needed and forwarded
none: no orbiting, no pinch-zoom, no double-tap to dive, on exactly the scales
where looking around **is** the interaction.

The layer was a wall.

> **A touch layer must add a control the scale does not have, and never
> intercept one it does.**

So the zones are scale-aware: they take the glass only where there is no camera
controller under it, and everywhere else the canvas keeps its events and the
layer contributes chrome alone.

## 3 · What was built — `src/touch.js`

**Nothing at rest.** Left half: an analog stick that materialises exactly where
the thumb lands and dissolves on release — magnitude and direction, written
straight into `input.js`'s axis. Right half: direct look-drag, with tap-to-
interact and double-tap-to-dive preserved. Pinch stays altitude.

**One button that blooms.** §6 M7 wants controls ≤14% of the screen, inside the
bottom 30%, faded after 3 s. The old layer answered with seven permanent
buttons. This answers with **one**: tap it for the scale's main verb, hold it and
the scale's other verbs fan out on an arc inside the same thumb sweep. Seven
affordances, one at rest, and none of them a row of icons the eye has to parse.

**Chrome cut to three persistent elements** (§3), all fading to zero at 4 s of no
input and returning on any input. The fade timer lives in `input.js`, which is
now the only thing that knows what "input" means.

## 4 · Validation — `tools/touchgate.js`

§6 M7's gate is almost entirely **DOM reads**, so unlike every other milestone
in this project it is fully decidable without a GPU. That is why it has its own
tool rather than a suite in `verify.js`.

Loads at **390 × 844** with touch and `deviceScaleFactor: 3`, and measures:

| §6 M7 clause | how |
|---|---|
| controls ≤ 14% of screen area | summed bounding boxes of what is *drawn* at rest |
| entirely within the bottom 30% | worst element top ≥ 70% of height |
| never co-present with keyboard hints | hint element absent whenever the layer is present |
| fades after 3 s idle | measured opacity, and the chrome at 4 s |
| any input brings it back | measured after a synthetic touch |
| all six scales controllable | per-scale zone behaviour, orbit vs walk |

Plus the clauses that are M7's actual substance rather than its budget: the stick
materialises where the thumb lands, **a half-pushed thumb writes half a unit of
movement** (the thing the old bridge destroyed), screen-up is forward, the zones
release the glass on self-steering scales and take it elsewhere, double-tap
dives, pinch reaches the scale as altitude, every verb the old seven-button row
had is still reachable, and **§8 axis 7 — `H` hides every last piece of chrome,
thumb layer included.**

## 5 · Gate (§6 M7)

> *At 390 × 844 — controls ≤ 14% of screen area, entirely within the bottom 30%,
> never co-present with keyboard hints, fade after 3 s idle, one-handed
> reachable. All six scales controllable. Mobile-tier budgets green.*

Everything but the last two is measured by `touchgate.js` and closed.

| clause | state |
|---|---|
| area, placement, fade, no keyboard hints, six scales | **closed** — DOM reads |
| one-handed reachable | **open** — a thumb is not a DOM read |
| **mobile-tier budgets green** | **open** — needs real glass, and `RECKONING` Act C has never measured the mobile tier with the grass on |

## 6 · Debt this plan records

- **M7 was never run on a phone.** Every clause above was measured in a desktop
  Chromium pretending to be one at 390 × 844. Emulation gets the layout right
  and says nothing about whether a thumb can reach the fan, or what the mobile
  tier's frame rate actually is.
- **The mobile §5 budget is unmeasured with the grass on**, exactly as the
  desktop one is — `RECKONING` Act C.
- The old rosette is still in `hud.js` behind `?m7=0`. It should be deleted once
  M7 is confirmed on real glass, and until then the file carries two touch
  layers.
