## 1 · What was there before

The surface character controller was 44 inline lines in `src/surface.js`, and
its entire vertical model was one line:

```js
body.y += (ground - body.y) * (1 - Math.exp(-12 * dt));
```

That is not a controller, it is a smoothing filter. No gravity, so no jump and
no fall. No slope limit, so a cliff was something you glided up at 16 m/s. No
step-up, because there was nothing to step over. §6 M4 asks for all of those by
name, plus coyote time, variable-height jump and momentum preservation.

Alongside it, three problems that are really one problem:

- **Seven input paths.** `main.js` routed, and four scales each kept a private
  duplicated `keys` Set, with no top-level `keyup` and no `blur` handler — so
  keys stuck after alt-tab.
- **Three sensitivities.** `surface.js`, `planetscale.js` and `blackhole.js`
  each rolled their own yaw/pitch drag at 0.0035, 0.0024 and 0.0040 rad/px, with
  three different pitch clamps. Nobody chose those. Looking around meant
  something slightly different depending on how far you had fallen, which is
  exactly the kind of thing a player feels and cannot name.
- **Mobile movement was four booleans.** `hud.js` synthesised `KeyboardEvent`s
  from a fixed rosette, so no analog magnitude survived anywhere in the touch
  path.

## 2 · What was built

### `src/input.js` — one action map, three sources

One shared state object exposing an **analog** `move` axis, a `look` delta and
named actions. A binding is defined once; keyboard, touch and gamepad are
*sources*, not handlers. Adds the two listeners that were missing — top-level
`keyup`, and `blur`/`visibilitychange` to release every held key.

**A named deviation from §6 M4**, recorded rather than smoothed over: the
milestone says *"rework input in `main.js`."* It became a new file instead,
because four scales cannot delete their duplicated `keys` Sets in favour of a
method on `main.js`, and the shared axis has to be importable by M7's touch
source.

`Space` could not become an ordinary binding: it belongs to pause-time globally
and a saved URL expects it to pause (§2.4). It takes the scale-first path
already established for `KeyB` — the active scale gets first refusal, and an
unhandled press still falls through to `togglePlay`.

### `src/avatar.js` — the controller, with no `three` import

Deliberately plain numbers over a `heightAt(x, z)` callback, so `tools/verify.js`
runs the whole thing in Node with no browser and no GPU. §6 M4's gate is mostly
about feel, which no test can score; the physics underneath it is entirely
decidable, and that is the half worth defending mechanically.

Constants that are not free choices: **eye 1.68 m** and **FOV 52** are §6 M4's
and are also the reference's own (`hoshi-no-tani.html:181-185`) — the two
documents agree to the digit, which is the tell that M4 is transcribing that
class. **Walk 3.45 m/s** is a human pace; the old 16 m/s was not a speed, it was
a way of crossing a 1400 m tile before the interest ran out, which is what M5's
skiff is actually for.

Gravity is `GM/R²` from the world's own mass and radius, and jump velocity is
derived from a target *height*, so a low-gravity moon launches you properly
rather than needing a per-world constant.

Vertical integration is **trapezoidal, not Euler**, and it is worth the extra
add: under constant acceleration `y += ½(v₀+v₁)·dt` is exact, where Euler leaves
13.5 mm short of the apex at 120 Hz. That is not a rounding error — it is a jump
that silently does not reach the height it was asked for, and it would have been
paid back by tuning `jumpHeight` until one machine looked right.

**One gait clock.** A single `stepPhase` drives head bob (2× step rate), lateral
sway (1×), roll, the footstep event, and — since M3 — the grass the walker
parts. §6 M4 asks for exactly this and gives the reason: *"so they can never
drift out of sync."* They cannot drift because there is nothing to drift from.

### `src/camera.js` — one rig

Implements the duck-typed `{ enabled, target, update() }` contract that
`surface.js:435` and `planetscale.js:828` already had, so `transition.js`'s
hyperzoom and every `enter/exit/resume` call site needed no changes.

Third person's spring arm is **swept**, which is the one clause M4's gate names.
`traveler.js`'s old arm clamped against the height *directly under the camera* —
a different question from whether anything sits between the camera and the head,
so walking backwards toward a cliff put the boom through it. The segment is now
marched at 12 samples over 4.6 m — a 38 cm resolution against a 55 cm clearance,
so the boom cannot pass through anything it could also stand on.

The arm eases its **length**, not its position. Easing the position lets the
camera drift through the wall it is being pushed out of.

## 3 · Offline validation (§7.3) — the `walk` suite

**32 checks**, all green. Each quantity gets a second, independent computation:

- **Trajectory replay** at fixed `dt` against closed forms — ballistic arcs
  against `v₀t − ½gt²`, the coyote window as an exact number of frames at three
  different `dt`, momentum preserved across a landing to the digit, the capsule
  never penetrating `makeGround()`'s height field.
- **§2.3** — the same trace produces a bit-identical trajectory on two runs.
- **Spring arm** — sampled along the route against `heightAt`, asserting the
  camera is never below ground + clearance and that the solve is continuous.
- **Gait clock** — footstep count over a fixed distance against the analytic
  count; bob and footfall derive from one phase and cannot desync.
- **Input latency** — the frame index between an event and the uniform write.

## 4 · Gate (§6 M4)

> *Input→visible response ≤ 2 frames; camera never clips terrain across the full
> route; a blind viewer of a 30 s third-person capture cannot identify a frame
> where control fights the camera.*

| clause | state |
|---|---|
| input → visible response ≤ 2 frames | **closed** — measured in the suite |
| camera never clips terrain across the route | **closed** — a CPU assertion over the sweep |
| no frame where control fights the camera | **open** — needs a human and 30 s of third-person capture |

The third clause is routed to `docs/GPU-RUN.md` with the rest. It is a judgement
and no instrument replaces it.

## 5 · What M4 did not do

- It converted **only `surface.js`** to the shared axis. `planetscale.js`,
  `system.js` and `clouds.js` still keep their own `keys` Sets. Converting four
  scales in one commit has no downstream to catch a regression.
- The three `OrbitControls` scales were not touched.
- `planetscale.js:1502`'s walk mode on the streaming globe is a **second,
  unrelated** controller in draw units. §2.6 forbids a float32 world position at
  planet scale, so the two paths keep their own coordinate handling and share
  only the logic.

inside the flag and became default in the flip commit, per §7.4 — but **no
milestone was re-shot and re-scored against them**, which §7.7 requires. That is
part of what `RECKONING` Act A exists to settle.
