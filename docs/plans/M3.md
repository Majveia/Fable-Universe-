## 1 · What this milestone actually is

Two files, one idea. **One global wind field, sampled by everything.** Grass,
foliage, dust, spores, cloth, water ripple, cloud advection, smoke — all of it
reads the same texture, so nothing in the frame can be blowing a different way
from anything else.

The grass is the visible half and the wind is the load-bearing half. A meadow
with perfect blades and incoherent motion reads as noise; a meadow with plain
blades and a coherent gust front reads as weather. §9.5's one-line doctrine:

> density is one continuous law, rings only switch tessellation, and everything
> that moves samples one wind field.

New files: `src/wind.js`, `src/flora.js`. Behind `?m3=1`, default-off (§7.4).

### 2.1 · The wind (§5, lines 1598–1830)

**Mean flow — Ornstein–Uhlenbeck meander.** `kS = 1 − exp(−dt/25)`,
`kD = 1 − exp(−dt/40)`; speed clamped to `[0.62, 1.45] ×` base, direction to
`± 0.34 rad` of base. Base is 4.2 m/s at the 10 m reference height, from 292°.

**Six coherent gust cells**, advected downwind at `1.25 ×` the mean flow.
Per cell: `len` 26–60 m, `wid` 70–200 m, `amp` 0.85–2.20, `veer` ± 0.21 rad.
Recycled when the head passes 620 m ahead of the camera, re-seeded 1560–1840 m
behind it. The profile, along-wind coordinate `u = (along − s)/len`:

```
skip unless −6.5 ≤ u ≤ 0.18
head  = smoothstep(0.15, 0.0, u)      ← the sharp leading edge
body  = exp(u · 2.05)                 ← the exponential tail behind it
cross = exp(−|(c⊥ − cell.c)/(wid/2)|^2.3)
g     = amp · head · body · cross
front = amp · exp(−|u| · 9.0) · cross ← what makes it read as a front
```

`front` is the term §6 M3 names by name. It is *not* the same as `g`: `g` is
the gust's strength, `front` is a thin ridge riding its leading edge, and it is
what the grass's wind-flash and the excitement channel key off.

**Inertial-subrange turbulence — four octaves of curl noise**, advected with the
flow (Taylor's frozen-turbulence hypothesis). `k₀ = 0.0118`, `ε = 0.021` for the
finite-difference curl, `k *= 2` and `amp *= 0.7937` per octave. That
multiplier is **2^(−1/3)**, which is Kolmogorov's −5/3 energy spectrum expressed
as a velocity amplitude — it is a physical constant, not a taste knob. Each
octave decorrelates on its own turnover time, `tt = t · 0.055 · 2^(i·0.667)`.

Curl noise rather than plain noise because `∇ × ψ` is divergence-free by
construction: air does not pile up.

**Terrain coupling.**
```
crest    = (h − mean4(h, ±58 m)) / 24
speedup  = 1 + 0.92 · clamp(crest, 0, 1.1)
shelter  = exp(−max(h_upwind(48 m) − h, 0) / 23)
speedup *= mix(0.42, 1.0, shelter)
contour deflection: fdir = normalize(mix(fdir, contour, clamp(slope·2.1, 0, 0.58)))
open water: speedup *= mix(1.0, 1.14, water)
```

**Logarithmic boundary layer**, `windProfile(z) = log((max(z, 0.015) + 0.06)/0.06) · 0.19523`.
Verified: at `z = 10` this returns 1.00000, so it is normalised to the 10 m
reference height exactly. Roots barely move, tips whip.

**The render target.** 440 m span, 160–352 px by tier. Output is
`vec4(velocity.xy, gustNorm, excite)` where `gustNorm = speed / max(meanSpeed, 0.4)`
and `excite = clamp(front·1.35 + turbMag·0.22, 0, 3)`.

**The analytic fallback and its edge mask** (lines 717–746). Beyond the target's
edge, three octaves of a travelling wave stand in, blended on
`edge = 1 − smoothstep(0.40, 0.498, max(|uv−0.5|))`. The early-out is the point:

```glsl
if (edge >= 0.999) return w;   // inside the RT, the fallback is pure waste
```

All of a blade's vertices sample one point, so this branch is perfectly coherent
across a warp. The reference calls it *"the single largest saving in the grass
vertex shader"*, and it is why gust bands still roll over hills 900 m away for
almost nothing.

### 2.2 · The grass (§9.5, lines 210–258)

**Four rings, and they exist only to switch tessellation.**

| ring | chunk | blades @ 1.0 | near–far | dn | wpx | hs | density at dn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 9 m | 89 000 | 0–26 m | 7 | 1.70 | 1.00 | 1100 /m² |
| 1 | 30 m | 177 000 | 22–84 m | 22 | 2.00 | 1.08 | 197 /m² |
| 2 | 100 m | 307 000 | 76–290 m | 76 | 2.75 | 1.36 | 31 /m² |
| 3 | 250 m | 231 000 | 260–1250 m | 260 | 4.00 | 1.95 | 3.7 /m² |

**One continuous density law:** `blades/m²(d) = B · min(1, (dn/d)^1.5)`, with
`K = B · dn^1.5` held constant so there is no step at any ring boundary.

**Exponent 1.5 exactly**, and not for taste: at 1.5 the shader evaluates it as
`x·x·inversesqrt(x)` — three single-cycle instructions against roughly ten for a
general `pow()` — on ~12 M vertices a frame.

**Thin twice.** Coarsely on the CPU by lowering a chunk's instance count from a
pre-shuffled buffer (any prefix is a fair sample, and a thinned blade costs
nothing — not even a vertex shader invocation), then finely per-blade in the
vertex shader against true distance, with the CPU deliberately over-drawing from
the chunk's **nearest** corner so the shader can only ever remove.

**The four quality rows**, adopted verbatim in shape (§5):

| tier | grass multipliers | shadow | wind RT | px | bloom | blade segments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 0.30 0.28 0.26 0.24 | 1280 | 160 | 0.85 | 4 | 3 1 1 1 |
| mid | 0.58 0.55 0.52 0.48 | 1536 | 224 | 1.00 | 5 | 3 2 1 1 |
| high | 1.00 1.00 1.00 1.00 | 2048 | 288 | 1.12 | 5 | 4 2 1 1 |
| ultra | 1.45 1.38 1.30 1.20 | 2560 | 352 | 1.32 | 6 | 5 3 2 1 |

Note the supersample discipline: `px` is **on top of** device pixel ratio and is
≥ 1.0 above low, so the composite always resolves *down* to the canvas.

## 3 · Three porting hazards, all confirmed by reading

**a · The reference's own density comment is stale.** The prose block at line
216 says the exponent is 1.45 and `K ≈ 17600`; the comment immediately below it
corrects to 1.5 with the `inversesqrt` argument, and `const DENS_POW = 1.5`.
**Port 1.5.** Anyone porting from the prose gets a subtly denser far field and a
different instruction count.

**b · Ring 3 breaks constant-K on purpose.** Computed from the table:

| ring | `B · dn^1.5` |
|---|---|
| 0 | 20 372 |
| 1 | 20 328 |
| 2 | 20 538 |
| 3 | **15 512** |

Rings 0–2 hold K to 1 %. Ring 3 is 24 % low — and the reference says why: *"what
actually has to stay constant is not the blade COUNT but the screen COVERAGE,
and coverage is density × width × height."* Its `wpx` rises 2.75 → 4.00, so it
trades count for width one-for-one. **The invariant to test is coverage, not
K** — a parity test written against constant-K would fail on a correct port.

*Measured during act 3, and it revises this paragraph:* the trade is
approximate, not one-for-one. Ring 3 keeps 0.762 of ring 2's `K` and widens
1.455, landing 11 % over; including the height scale as the reference's own
sentence suggests puts it 59 % over. `wpx` is an angular *floor* that only
binds past a distance, so no single product is conserved at a boundary. A
parity test against exact coverage continuity fails a correct port too. See
§11.3.

**c · `updateWind` violates §2.3 in eight places.** `Math.random()` at:
`tgtSpeed`, `tgtDir`, and six sites inside the cell-recycle block
(`r`, `c.c`, `c.len`, `c.wid`, `c.amp`, `c.veer`). Every one has to be reseeded.

This is not merely a compliance edit — the reference's version is also **frame-rate
dependent**, because it accumulates `Math.random() · dt` per frame, so a 30 fps
and a 60 fps run diverge. Driving the meander from a seeded, time-parameterised
noise function instead fixes both at once and is strictly better:

```js
tgtSpeed(t) = base · (1 + 0.42 · noise1(seed_s, t / 25))
tgtDir(t)   = baseDir + 0.34 · noise1(seed_d, t / 40)
```

Cell state becomes a pure function of `(index, generation)` where
`generation = floor((s₀ + advected) / recycleSpan)`. Same statistics, no
accumulator, and the same seed gives the same weather forever (§2.3).

## 4 · What is AEON's rather than the reference's

The reference is one meadow under one star on one world. Five things cannot
port as literals, and each gets the §16.3 treatment — *port the function that
produced the number, not the number*.

**a · Wind speed is a property of the world.** 4.2 m/s at 10 m is a temperate
Earth afternoon. AEON derives it from what actually sets surface wind: the
equator-to-pole temperature gradient driving the pressure gradient, damped by
surface friction and turned by Coriolis. All of the inputs exist on `pp` already
(`Teq`, `radiusE`, `massE`, rotation, `atmo`). An airless world has no wind at
all and the whole system must fall out at zero rather than be branched around —
that is the check that the parameterisation is right and not fitted, exactly as
`aerialParams()` was.

**b · The terrain coupling needs a height field in GLSL.** The reference has a
1280² height texture as its single source of truth. AEON's surface heights live
in vertex positions and in `ground.heightAt()` on the CPU; there is no GLSL
height function at surface scale. The clean answer is to **bake a height texture
over the wind RT's own span** — 440 m at 256² is 1.7 m per texel, far finer than
anything the coupling terms vary by (the crest filter is ±58 m, the shelter
lookup 48 m). One bake at init, deterministic, and it makes §2.7's discipline
hold here too: one definition, sampled two ways.

**c · `splatAt`'s water mask** becomes `h < seaLevel`, which AEON already has.

**d · Grass exists where grass grows.** The reference has a hand-authored meadow
mask. AEON has `material.js`'s four-layer blend, and the `sward` weight already
answers "is this ground grassy" from slope × altitude × latitude × moisture.
**Flora reads the material blend rather than growing a second opinion** — the
same reason `ground.js` exists.

**e · The palette is per-world.** §9.5's blade detail — teal root to yellow-green
tip, four patch colours, dry patches — ports as a *structure* filled from the
world's own palette via `material.js`, not as nine hex literals.

## 5 · Files, and the boundary between them

| file | owns | may not import |
|---|---|---|
| `src/wind.js` | the field: cells, meander, turbulence, coupling, the RT pass, the GLSL sampler, and the CPU mirror | `three` — so `tools/verify.js` can test it in node, the same rule `avatar.js`, `ocean.js` and `horizon.js` follow |
| `src/flora.js` | rings, chunks, the instance buffers, the blade geometry and its vertex/fragment shaders | — |

`wind.js` exporting no-three keeps the CPU mirror and the GLSL provably the same
arithmetic (§7.3), which matters more here than anywhere else in the project:
the audio, the dust, the camera shake and the grass all read the mirror, and a
mirror that has drifted from the field is §2.7's failure in a new costume.

The RT itself is built in `flora.js` (or `surface.js`) from `wind.js`'s exported
GLSL and uniform block — the same split `aerial.js` / `surface.js` already use.

## 6 · Offline validation, before any of it renders (§7.3)

This container has no GPU, so the closeable gates are the offline ones. A new
`wind` suite and a new `flora` suite in `tools/verify.js`:

**wind**
- `windProfile(10) == 1.0` to 1e-9, and the profile is monotonic in `z`.
- The turbulence cascade's amplitude ratio is `2^(-1/3)` to 1e-12, and the
  summed spectrum has the −5/3 slope over its four octaves when fitted in log-log.
- Curl noise is divergence-free: `∇ · v` sampled on a 64² grid by central
  differences is within 1e-6 of zero.
- A gust front crossing a fixed point produces a **single** `front` maximum with
  a rise time shorter than its decay time (the leading edge is sharp, the tail
  is not) — the property §6 M3 asks for, as a measurement.
- Terrain coupling: speed-up over a synthetic ridge, shelter in its lee, and
  deflection along its contours, each against a hand-computed value.
- **§2.3** — the same seed and the same `t` gives a bit-identical field on two
  runs, and the field is independent of the `dt` schedule used to reach `t`
  (three different step sizes, same answer). This is the test the reference's
  own implementation would fail.
- **§2.7 parity** — the GLSL and the CPU mirror agree to 2/255 over 10⁴ samples.

**flora**
- The density law is continuous across all four ring boundaries: sampled either
  side of 22, 76 and 260 m, the density agrees to 1 %.
- **Coverage**, not K, is what holds across ring 3 (hazard b): `density × wpx ×
  hs` continuous to 5 % at the 260 m boundary.
- `(dn/d)^1.5` and `x·x·inversesqrt(x)` agree to float precision.
- Any prefix of the shuffled instance buffer is a fair spatial sample:
  χ² on a 16 × 16 occupancy grid, at 10 %, 50 % and 90 % prefixes.
- The CPU's over-draw uses the chunk's nearest corner, so the shader's
  per-blade test can only ever remove: assert `cpuCount ≥ shaderKeeps` over a
  grid of camera positions, with no position where a blade the shader wants is
  absent.
- **Un-grassed annuli (§11)** — the chunk grid for each ring is derived from
  that ring's own far distance, and the test walks the camera outward and
  asserts grass coverage never has a gap. The reference records this exact bug.

## 7 · Gate (§6 M3)

- ≥ 800 k blades ≥ 60 fps desktop; proportionate count ≥ 30 fps mobile.
- No visible density step at any ring boundary.
- A gust crosses frame as a coherent front with a legible leading edge.

## 9 · Budgets at risk (§5)

Every one of them. This is the milestone that spends the frame.

- **Triangles.** 800 k blades at 3–5 segments is 4.8–8 M triangles before
  thinning — against §5's 2.2 M ceiling at surface scale. The thinning *is* the
  budget, which is why acts 3 and 4 are separate: act 3 has to demonstrate the
  law before act 4 multiplies it by four.
- **Draw calls.** Ring 3's 250 m chunks over a 1250 m far distance is a 10 × 10
  grid — 100 draw calls for one ring. Against §5's 900 ceiling, four rings must
  come in well under half of it or the LOD is wrong.
- **CPU.** The per-chunk instance-count thinning runs every frame on every
  chunk. §5's 12 ms main-thread budget is the constraint, and the pre-shuffled
  buffer is what makes it a count assignment rather than a sort.
- **GPU memory.** Instance buffers for ~1.2 M blades at 32 B is 38 MB, plus the
  wind RT and the height bake. Comfortable against 400 MB desktop, tight against
  220 MB mobile once the quality row scales the counts.

§5's rule stands: *any change that costs frames must pay for them. Add the LOD
before the feature.* Here the LOD **is** the feature, which is the only reason
this milestone is affordable at all.

## 11 · Acts 1–4, as built

What §8's sequence planned, and what actually happened. Recorded because three
of the four acts corrected something — twice something the reference had wrong
about itself, twice something I had wrong about my own previous act.

### 11.1 · Act 1 · The field — `src/wind.js`

Built stateless. Every quantity is a pure function of `(seed, t, position)`,
which kills §2.3's entropy ban and a genuine defect in the reference at once:
`updateWind` accumulates `Math.random() · dt`, so the variance of its random
walk grows with the number of *steps* rather than elapsed time, and a 30 fps
machine and a 120 fps machine drift into different weather inside a minute.
Three suite checks would fail on the reference.

The six recycled gust cells became an **infinite lattice** — cell `j` at
`j·LANE` plus hash-derived jitter, no recycling, no camera dependence. Two
observers a kilometre apart share one sky.

**Two constants derived rather than copied, and both caught something:**

| | derived | the reference |
|---|---|---|
| log-profile normaliser | `1/log((10+z₀)/z₀)` = **0.19523708** | hardcoded `0.19523` |
| gust-cell lattice period | **260 m** | `i*430` — but that is only where its six cells *start* |

The second was the interesting one. In steady state each of the reference's
cells travels a 1560 m span before recycling, so six cells run 260 m apart.
Porting the 430 gave a field gusting 3 % of the time — still air with occasional
events. Implementing its own cells with its own recycle rule and running them to
steady state measures **4.2 %**; the lattice reproduces 4.4 %, and a gust sits in
a 200 m frame 29 % of the time against its 25 %. The first version of that test
asserted "roughly a tenth", which was a guess and was wrong.

**Wind speed is the world's**, through §9.6's discipline. Earth comes out at
**4.12 m/s against the reference's 4.2**, with the textbook 0.37 friction
fraction. Within 2 % from an independent chain is evidence; tuning the fraction
to hit 4.2 exactly would destroy the only thing that makes it evidence.

That chain produces a result worth stating because it is surprising and correct:
**the geostrophic speed is independent of surface pressure** — `p` cancels
between the gradient and the density. Thinning the air does not slow the wind;
Mars is the demonstration. So `windForceScale()` carries the pressure dependence
separately, with a Knudsen ramp where the flow stops being a fluid at all. An
airless world has a nominal wind and nothing to push with, and the grass stands
still without a branch anywhere.

### 11.2 · Act 2 · The height bake — `src/flora.js`

192² over ±1400 m: a 14.7 m texel against a ±58 m crest filter and a 48 m
shelter lookup. 512² would cost 1.2 s at load against §5's 2.5 s to interactive,
for detail the coupling cannot use. The gradient stencil widened from the
reference's ±7 m to one texel, because a finer difference on a 14.7 m table
reads interpolation rather than terrain.

The suite compares the coupling **terms**, not the heights, because that is the
claim. Speed-up survives to 0.077 across a range spanning 0.42–2.01.

**And it found something that was not a bake artefact.** The reference deflects
flow along a *signed* contour — `perp(grad)`, flipped by `dot(contour, fwd) < 0`.
A contour line has no sign, and that test is bistable exactly where a hillside
faces upwind, which is common. Two texels with near-identical gradients deflect
180° apart, and the field grows a seam along every upwind slope. Measured, the
worst disagreement was a full reversal at maximum weight.

Damping the up-slope *component* has no sign to choose:

```
dir' = normalize(dir − w · n · (dir · n))
```

Air prefers to go around a slope rather than over it. Continuous everywhere, one
fewer branch, the physics the reference was reaching for. Worst disagreement is
now 0.293 of a 0.58 maximum, and it is interpolation error rather than a
discontinuity.

### 11.3 · Act 3 · The law and one ring — `src/meadow.js`, `src/flora.js`

§3's three hazards all confirmed by measurement rather than assumed. Ring 3 is
**76.2 %** of ring 2's `K`; the count-for-width trade lands **11 %** over, or
**59 %** with the height scale — so it is a rationale rather than an identity,
and a parity test written against constant `K` *or* exact coverage continuity
fails a correct port.

Blades per steradian **rise** with distance — 287k at 200 m to 498k at 600 m —
which is the whole trick and why the horizon reads as meadow rather than as a
green plane.

**Three faults in my own first draft**, the first of which would have shipped
looking correct:

- **The shuffle was a no-op.** Roots from `hash(seed, i)` are already
  decorrelated from their index, so every prefix was fair and the shuffle bought
  nothing — a line that looks load-bearing and is not. Roots are now
  **stratified**, which correlates index with position and is exactly what makes
  the shuffle necessary. An unshuffled 30 % prefix reaches 2.7 m of a 9 m chunk.
- **`instanceCount` lives on the geometry, not the mesh.** One shared geometry
  meant every chunk drew the same count.
- Dead code: a lattice root assignment immediately overwritten.

### 11.4 · Act 4 · Four rings

§11's un-grassed annuli is now a measurement: 29,696 probes across four rings,
four stances inside the home chunk, and thirty-two azimuths, asking whether
every point inside a ring's band is covered by a chunk that ring actually draws.
The four bands cover 0–1250 m unbroken.

**Two corrections to act 3's own reasoning, both mine:**

- Act 3 dismissed frustum culling as *"a second, weaker answer to a question

- And the fix over-claimed. I wrote "roughly four chunks in five" before

### 11.5 · What the instrument caught, and why it could

`shadercheck`'s bench route is a continuous flight between scales — right on
real silicon, unreachable on a software rasteriser. Every pass spent its whole
600 s timeout in the black hole and reported `scalesVisited: ["blackhole"]`:
*"0 failures across 88 shaders"* about scales it never visited.

`--stations` navigates each scale's deep link directly. Its own first version
was **also** a false green — `addInitScript` re-runs per navigation, so
`window.__AEON_SHADERS` is fresh per station and reading it once at the end
reports the last station's set (24 shaders) as the whole run.

Harvesting per station fixed it, and **the first run that ever reached the
surface scale went red**, twice over:

1. `RawShaderMaterial` gets no preamble from three. The full-screen quad used
   `position` and `uv` undeclared. That two-second check now lives in
   `parse.js`, verified by reintroducing the bug.
2. A `uniform2fv` TypeError I assumed was a cascade, and was not. The wind
   *system* was reaching a `vec2` uniform: `_buildMeadow` had assigned over
   `surface.js`'s long-standing `this.wind` vector, read by `grass.js`'s petals,
   `weather.js`'s rain slant and a `uWind` uniform. Nothing threw — `s.wind.x`
   became `undefined` and NaN went quietly into two unrelated systems.

**And that collision exposed something worse than itself.** `makeWind` was
minting its own prevailing direction from a hash while `surface.js` already had
one — **two winds in one world**, which is the single thing §6 M3 exists to
prevent. The failure would have been a frame where the grass leans one way and
the rain falls the other: visible, wrong, and nearly impossible to attribute to
a hash in a constructor.

Finally, the gate learned not to die over a cosmetic string: the renderer-name
probe raced a navigation (§2.4 — the app rewrites its address as scales change)
and took a complete run down with it. It is taken from the first station now,
and guarded.

### 11.6 · Where acts 5 and 6 stand

Not started. §9.5's blade detail — the rolled cross-section, the root-to-tip hue
path, tussock clustering at metre and decametre scales, the four-colour meadow
mosaic, dry patches, the wind flash — is act 5, and act 3 deliberately shipped a
single hue path and per-blade variation rather than a sketch of it, so that
act 5 has a before and after worth looking at.

Act 6 — everything else sampling the field — is the milestone's actual thesis
and the act most likely to be cut for time. It should not be.
